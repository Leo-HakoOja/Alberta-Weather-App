# Product name: "Alberta Weather"

The app ships as **Alberta Weather** on both stores. We rejected brand-name alternatives ("Chinook," "Foothills," "Myrnam Weather") in favour of the descriptive name.

## Why

A solo-dev free app has exactly two marketing channels: word-of-mouth from friends, and App Store search. Word-of-mouth works with any name. App Store search rewards descriptive exact-match names. An Albertan typing "alberta weather" into the App Store search box must find this app on the first row — that lookup is the entire acquisition funnel.

Brand-name alternatives ("Chinook," "Foothills") would put us behind a discovery wall that only a marketing budget can scale. They'd also gain us little: the brand experience lives in the icon, the screenshots, the subtitle/tagline, and the in-app design language (see [brand-alberta.md](../brand-alberta.md)) — not in the name itself. Apple's own weather app is called "Weather"; the brand is the experience, not the wordmark.

Tagline candidate from grilling: *"a certain beauty in Alberta weather."* That's where the romance lives.

## Consequences

- The App Store *subtitle* (30 chars) becomes load-bearing — it carries the brand voice the name doesn't.
- The repo directory stays `weather-app`. The product name and the codebase name are not coupled.
- If a competing "Alberta Weather" app appears on the store later, this decision needs revisiting — exact-match search becomes a tie rather than a slam-dunk.
- App Store and Google Play name clearance must be verified before submission. If "Alberta Weather" is taken in a way that materially harms search ranking, supersede this ADR.
